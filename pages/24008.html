Three Functions of Evaporative Emissions System 

  • Capture fuel vapors and stores them in a charcoal canister.
  • Deliver stored fuel vapors to the engine to burn.
  • ECM performs EVAP Self-Test

Self-Test 

During the EVAP System self-test:

  • ECM closes the Canister Close Valve (CCV) at the charcoal canister to seal off the evaporative emission system.
  • ECM opens the Purge Control Solenoid Valve (PCSV) through duty cycle control to generate vacuum in the fuel tank with vehicle.
  • The ECM monitors the Fuel Tank Pressure Sensor (FTPS) voltage during the EVAP self-test and sets a corresponding DTC, if the FTPS response is outside of an expected PASS range.

Common DTCs: 

  • P0456, P0455, P0449, P1326
  • Refer to DTC  service information for current leak check information.

SERVICE PROCEDURE

  1. Refer to Service Manual for DTC Description.
  2. Place tool on LEVEL surface, such as a work bench or a tool cart.
  3. Attach the power cable from the 110V port to a power outlet.
  4. Attach the test hose to the on-board air supply port.
  5. Perform Tool Calibration.
    • Set the Flow/Decay Control lever (A) and Pump Power switch (B) to either Vacuum or Pressure  setting. (Determine which testing method is best for the specific EVAP leak condition.)
    • Dead head the test hose with system block off adapter to verify vacuum does not exceed 18-20in. H2O on the Vacuum/Pressure Gauge.
  6. For most situations, the 0.020 (0.50mm) standard is the acceptable leak standard to begin the EVAP system leak testing.
    • Attach test hose to the 0.020 (0.50 mm) calibration test port to begin the tool calibration.
    NOTE: In certain situations where the EVAP system must be tested for very small leaks not detectable by the 0.020 (0.50 mm) standard, use the 0.010 (0.25 mm) calibration method for further diagnosis.
  7. Rotate the Vacuum/Pressure Calibration knob to "fine tune" calibrate while referencing the vacuum/pressure gauge to the selected leak standard from above step. Depending on Vacuum or Pressure setting, the corresponding airflow ball level will then float to a specific area.
    • Slide the red reference arrow (up/down) to line up with the floating ball on the airflow level gauge.

  8. Information 

    Do NOT  test the EVAP system from the fuel filler neck. The fuel tank has an inlet check valve that will prevent accurate airflow and will result in the EVAP system NOT  testing properly.

    • Connect test hose to vehicle EVAP system, such as from the CCV to apply test air towards the EVAP Canister to check for system sealing.
    • Set the Flow/Decay Control lever and Pump Power Switch to either the Vacuum or Pressure  setting. (Begin initially with tool set to Vacuum, since the vehicle performs the EVAP self-test this way.)
    • Leak testing should begin from the rear of the vehicle, starting from where the canister close valve (Normally OPEN  ) connects to the charcoal canister.
      • Systematically test all the branches of the EVAP system towards purge control solenoid valve (Normally CLOSED  ) at the front of the vehicle.
    • You can also use the Open/Close valves of the EVAP test manifold connected between the charcoal canister and the fuel tank to isolate and inspect the specific branches of the EVAP system to test for a leak.
      • Once the leak is detected and isolated to that specific branch, use the connection adapters to individually test each component of that branch to pinpoint the source of the leak.
  9. Pull sufficient vacuum in EVAP system until airflow level ball bottoms out in the airflow gauge.

  10. Information 

    If the airflow meter ball cannot descend below vehicles governed leak standard threshold, it will indicate FAIL.

    • Vacuum/Pressure gauge should read 18-20 in. H2O.
    • Set Flow/Decay Control lever to Decay, then observe Vacuum/Pressure Gauge and watch for the level degradation, indicating a leak.
    • At this point, turn the Pump Power switch to Off/Decay.
  11. If the level degradation is indicated, isolate the source of leak.
    • Use the included selection of fittings/hose to selectively test the sealing integrity of the EVAP circuit branches and systematically seeking out individual components.
    • By using the process of elimination, the exact source of the EVAP leak can be determined.
    • It may be possible to visually find the leak point by using the pressure setting of the tool and soapy water.
  12. Perform the necessary repairs according to the above diagnosis, then retest the system to confirm the repair.
