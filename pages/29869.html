WEBDCS DEALER AGED INVENTORY - BLUE LINK SOC ALERTS

WEBDCS DEALER AGED INVENTORY - BLUE LINK SOC ALERTS

TECHNICAL SERVICE BULLETIN

Reference Number(s): 24-EE-013H, Date of Issue:  October 01, 2024
HYUNDAI: All Models
GROUP: ENGINE ELECTRICAL
Superceded Bulletin(s): 20-EE-003H, Date of Issue:  October 01, 2024

REVISION HISTORY

This TSB supersedes TSB # 20-EE-003H to include updated battery testing information and to reflect Bluelink® as a standard feature on newer vehicles. 

IMPORTANT: *****DEALER STOCK ONLY*****
Battery maintenance to be performed on all affected vehicles notified on WebDCS "Aged Inventory - Blue Link Alerts"  with either a Low Battery SOC alert (less than 50%) or 30 Day alert.
This bulletin is a supplement to TSB # 24-EE-014H  , "12V Battery Maintenance for Inventory and Pre-Delivery Inspections (PDI)  ", for all connected vehicles reporting Battery SOC through Bluelink.

DESCRIPTION

This bulletin provides information on identifying dealer stock vehicles with either:

  • A battery SOC alert - low battery State of Charge (SOC) at or below 50%.

    Or 

  • A 30 Day alert - vehicle has not started and reported an SOC in 30 days.

Alerted VINs can be found on the WebDCS Aged Inventory - Blue Link Alerts  dashboard in both the Sales  and Service  tabs.

  • The information in the WebDCS dashboard is based on the vehicle's calculated Battery SOC and is transmitted via Bluelink at every key OFF  , engine OFF  event.
  • Proper battery maintenance is required to extend battery life. Leaving batteries in a discharged state for extended time below 50% SOC will negatively reduce battery health and impact customer satisfaction (CSI).
  • Vehicles are equipped with a suite of advanced safety and convenience features that depend on a healthy battery for full functionality.
  • Battery maintenance should be started immediately  on alerted vehicles.
  • Charging of the battery and verifying SOC is outlined in this TSB.

Applicable Vehicles: 

  • All Dealer Stock vehicles except hybrid electric models equipped with Lithium 12V batteries.
NOTE: Per HMA warranty policy, batteries that fail during storage after 7 days from arrival are the dealer's responsibility.

SERVICE PROCEDURE

IDENTIFYING VEHICLES WITH A LOW BATTERY SOC ALERT OR 30 DAY ALERT

  1. Access Hyundai Motor America's WebDCS.
  2. Select either the Sales  or Service  tab.
  3. Scroll down and select Aged Inventory - Blue Link Alerts  .
    Sales Service
    NOTE: The number indicated in the red circle within the subcategory Aged Inventory - Blue Link Alerts  denotes how many VINs require battery maintenance or have not been started (have not sent SOC data) in 30 days.
  4. A list of dealer stock vehicles in both Sales  and Service  tabs with low battery SOC  alert and 30 DAYS  alert will be displayed, which will require immediate attention.

SOC ALERT

  1. Locate the vehicles with the lowest battery SOC first.
    NOTE: Handling these vehicles first is a top priority. They are more likely to fail while on your lot if not treated as soon as possible.
  2. Perform battery charging using either of the methods outlined in Low Battery SOC Alert Maintenance - How to Raise Battery SOC  in this TSB.
  3. After performing battery maintenance, provide the appropriate status update for each individual VIN through the Aged Inventory - Blue Link Alerts  in either the Sales  or Service  Tabs.

    Information 

    Refer to the chart below for definitions of each Status update choice.
Status Definition
In Progress 
  • Actions are in place to perform the battery maintenance before end of current business day.
Complete 
  • Actions have been taken to sufficiently charge the vehicle battery.
Sold 
  • The vehicle has been sold to a customer and is no longer in dealer stock.
Requirement Not Met 
  • This is a system generated  status due to the following:
    • The vehicle was unable to achieve the proper SOC.
    • The proper steps were not completed to transmit the updated SOC

    Refer to Notice on page 7 for further details on transmitting the new SOC. 

  • No actions were taken from the previous day.
  • Incorrect status selection from previous day.

Information 

  • VINs will be automatically removed from WebDCS once the vehicle properly reports an SOC value of greater than 80%. No further action will be required for the status update column in WebDCS.
  • The VIN will automatically repopulate to WebDCS if the SOC value is equal to or less than 50% from the last reported transmitted data.

30 DAY ALERT

  1. Locate the vehicle in dealer inventory with a 30 DAY  alert from WebDCS.
  2. Start the vehicle and run the engine for approximately 30 seconds so a new SOC% can be sent to WebDCS. This will clear the 30 DAY  alert automatically.
  3. While the vehicle is running, verify that the SOC level is above  50% as outlined in Verifying Battery SOC  in this TSB. If the SOC level is below  50%, an SOC Alert will be displayed in WebDCS.
  4. A vehicle with an SOC at or below 50% requires charging. Follow the guidelines below to charge the battery above 50% SOC.
NOTE: It is highly recommended to charge above 80%  SOC level to reverse battery sulfation and minimize re-alerts or the need for recharging.

LOW BATTERY SOC ALERT MAINTENANCE - HOW TO RAISE BATTERY SOC

Use either option A  or B  below to charge the battery.

  1. Let engine idle with headlights ON  .
    NOTE: Use this method if battery SOC > 30%.
    1. Perform a diagnostic charge with the Midtronics DCA-8000 
      NOTE: This is a Hyundai Essential Tool.
    2. Perform the Automatic Regulated Battery Charger Procedures  on page 6  in this TSB.

      Information 

      For more details on the Midtronics DCA 8000  refer to TSB # 24-EE-001H.

AUTOMATIC REGULATED BATTERY CHARGER PROCEDURES

  1. Connect the positive (red) charge clamp (A) to the jumper plate or tab (B) of the battery positive (+) terminal.
  2. The negative (black) charge clamp (C) must be connected to the metal plate (D) between the battery sensor and chassis ground cable, or at an unpainted chassis ground bolt, not  directly to the battery terminal.

    Information 

    Refer to diagram at right for more details on connecting the negative (black) charge clamp.
  3. Close all doors and close the hood (or compress the striker to simulate hood close). Lock the vehicle and place the keys at least 10 ft away from vehicle.
  4. After charging time is met, run vehicle briefly for 10 seconds and turn it OFF  to allow the transmission of battery SOC to WebDCS updating the Aged Inventory - Blue Link Alerts  .
NOTE: If any of these conditions occur after charging: 
  • Re-alerts to WebDCS stating "Requirement Not Met  "
  • Instrument cluster SOC or GDS SOC indicates "- -" or "checking  " or "255  "
  • GDS Battery Diagnosis SOC = invalid 

Possible causes: 
  • Charging from a deeply discharged battery (29-0% SOC).
  • Charging directly at the battery terminals rather than with the charger black clamp connected at the plate or tab before the vehicle's battery sensor.
  • Charging after disconnecting battery cable(s) or removing the battery to be charged outside of the vehicle.

Then the following steps are needed to recalibrate SOC and transmit data to WebDCS: 
  1. Vehicle needs to sleep with ignition OFF  for at least 4 hours, with doors, hood, and trunk closed and vehicle locked with keys placed at least 10 feet away from vehicle.
  2. Perform an engine ON/OFF  event with at least 10 seconds of engine running.

VERIFYING BATTERY SOC

The vehicle's battery SOC may be verified by using either option A  or B  below based on which applies to the vehicle and is convenient for each dealer situation.

  1. Instrument Cluster Battery SOC Display:
    • Battery SOC can be viewed on the instrument cluster with the ignition switch turned ON  for new vehicles in Shipping Mode.
    • Vehicles with over 100 miles and/or removed from Shipping Mode will not display battery SOC on the instrument cluster.
    NOTE: While battery is being charged with a charger, the instrument cluster SOC can be monitored by turning the ignition ON  briefly. DO NOT  leave the ignition ON  for an extended period of time.
  2. GDS Current Data:

    State of Charge of Battery (AMS)  is one of the parameters found in the Engine Data Analysis  screens in GDS Smart.

    NOTE: In the event that a battery fails to take a charge or hold a charge, perform battery diagnostics according to TSB # 24-EE-003H-1  , "Guide to Battery Diagnosis TSBs
