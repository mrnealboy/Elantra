EVAP DUAL PURGE EJECTOR ASSEMBLY INFORMATION P04F00

EVAP DUAL PURGE EJECTOR ASSEMBLY INFORMATION P04F00

TECHNICAL SERVICE BULLETIN

Reference Number(s): 24-EC-001H, Date of Issue:  July 01, 2024
HYUNDAI: 2021 and later Elantra (CN7); 2022 - 2023 Kona (OS); 2024 and later Kona (SX2); 2022 and later Santa Cruz (NXT); 2021 - 2023 Santa Fe (TMA); 2024 and later Santa Fe (MX5A); 2021 and later Sonata (DN8)
GROUP: EMISSION CONTROL

DESCRIPTION

This bulletin provides information on the Dual Purge Ejector Assembly which is part of the EVAP emission control system on vehicles with turbocharged GDI engines.

To meet more stringent recent EVAP regulations some Hyundai vehicles sold in North America are equipped with the Dual Purge Ejector Assembly starting in 2021. The Dual Purge Ejector is used on turbocharged engines to allow for purging fuel vapor emissions stored in the canister during boost conditions (under load).

In many cases the shop manual may not contain enough information for some models equipped with the Dual Purge Ejector Assembly. Refer to this TSB for details on the Dual Purge Ejector Assembly including: overall system description, operation, components, DTCs, and diagnostic information.

APPLICABLE VEHICLES

  • 2021MY and later Elantra (CN7) equipped with 1.6L T-GDI engine
  • 2022MY - 2023MY Kona (OS) equipped with 1.6L T-GDI engine
  • 2024MY and later Kona (SX2) equipped with 1.6L T-GDI engine
  • 2022MY and later Santa Cruz (NXT) equipped with 2.5L T-GDI engine
  • 2021MY - 2023MY Santa Fe (TMA) equipped with 2.5L T-GDI engine
  • 2024MY and later Santa Fe (MX5A) equipped with 2.5L T-GDI engine
  • 2021MY and later Sonata (DN8) equipped with 2.5L T-GDI engine

On the following page is a basic diagram of the EVAP Dual Purge Ejector Assembly system including the major components and their place within the EVAP emission control system.

EVAP Dual Purge Ejector Assembly 

Major Components: 

  • Ejector Assembly: 

    Used on turbocharged engines to create a vacuum in the EVAP purge line during boost conditions from the Purge Control Solenoid Valve (PCSV) to the fresh air intake system.

    Ejector location varies depending on model, but is in the fresh air intake system, generally mounted in the fresh air inlet hose between the air filter and the turbocharger inlet.

  • Ejector Pressure Sensor (some models): 

    In some models the Ejector Assembly is equipped with a pressure sensor that provides the ECM with a pressure signal through the ejector.

  • Recirculation Flow Line: 

    During boost conditions, boost pressure flows through the recirculation line and into a venturi inside the Ejector creating a vacuum in the EVAP purge line, through the PCSV and to the intake air inlet to the turbocharger.

  • Check Valves: 

There are two check valves in most vehicles, some are equipped with three. In the diagram above, there are two check valves after a Tee in the EVAP line below the PCSV, plus a third check valve (CV3) on the recirculation flow line.

  • Check Valve 1 (CV1): Located in the purge line between the Intake manifold and the tee on the outlet side of the PCSV. CV1 opens under normal engine vacuum conditions and closes during boost conditions to prevent boost from entering the purge line and pressurizing hydrocarbons where they may be forced out of the EVAP system into the atmosphere through the EVAP canister vent.
  • Check Valve 2 (CV2): Located between the tee on the outlet side of the PCSV and the Ejector. During boost conditions, CV2 opens, and the purge vapor is drawn through the EVAP ejector into the intake air tube, through the turbocharger and intercooler, to the intake manifold.
  • Check Valve 3 (CV3): When present, it is located on the recirculation flow line and permits flow in only one direction.

SYSTEM OPERATION

Condition: No Load, Purging during Vacuum 

In the example above the driving conditions are with no load and purging is taking place with a vacuum in the intake manifold which is drawing the vapors from the canister into the intake manifold after the throttle plate.

This example shows 3 check valves:

  • CV1 is open because there is a vacuum on that side of the purge line.
  • CV2 is closed because there is no vacuum
  • CV3 closes when boost pressure is vented as the throttle closes.

Condition: High Load, Purging during Boost 

In the example above the driving conditions are with a load and purging is taking place. During boost, pressure enters the recirculation flow line and passes through the ejector creating a vacuum in the purge line which can draw vapors from the canister into the fresh air intake of the turbocharger.

This example shows 3 check valves:

  • CV1 is closed because there is boost pressure in the intake.
  • CV2 is open because there is a vacuum on that side of the purge line from the ejector.
  • CV3 is open as boost passes through it to the ejector.

COMPONENT DETAILS

EJECTOR

The Ejector is a siphoning device located on the fresh air intake between the air filter and the turbocharger inlet. It uses boost pressure that is passed through a nozzle (venturi) to create a low-pressure area (vacuum) which can draw vapors from the canister during acceleration conditions.

The diagram below shows the main parts of the Ejector assembly:

  • Inlet nozzle (venturi)
  • Diffuser (mixes boost with fuel vapor from the canister)
  • Discharge outlet

EJECTOR PRESSURE SENSOR (EP SENSOR)

Some vehicles have ejectors that are equipped with a pressure sensor which provides the ECM or PCM a pressure feedback signal during boost conditions to verify purge operation under load. On vehicles without the Ejector Pressure Sensor, the ECM uses the Fuel Tank Pressure Sensor for monitoring Dual Purge Ejector operation under load conditions.

DTCS AND DIAGNOSTIC INFORMATION

DTC P04F00 - EVAP System High Load Purge Line "A" Performance: 

This example shows the DTC information from a 2023 Kona (OS) 1.6L T-GDI Engine.

It's important to note the Information (A) at the top of the page below the DTC Description which directs you to inspect for debris inside the Ejector.

Also, refer to the Possible Cause (B)  points listed under DTC Setting Condition.

Examples of the most common causes for this DTC in all models are listed and include the following:

  • Clogged or restricted ejector due to debris
  • Clogged or restricted purge line due to debris
  • Clogged or restricted recirculation line due to debris
  • Ejector or purge line hoses leaking, or installed incorrectly (swapped)
  • Fuel vapor line hoses leaking, disconnected, or plugged

Another important item to check that is NOT  listed here are the check valves:

  • Inspect for leaking check valve(s). They should pass air in only one direction.
  • Verify the check valves are installed in the correct direction.

Troubleshooting Example 1: DTC P04F00 

Below is an example of a Dual Purge Ejector on a Santa Fe (TMA) with a 2.5L T-GDI engine that was plugged with debris which resulted in DTC P04F00. The circled area (C) indicates where the debris was located when viewing the discharge side of the Ejector at the air cleaner mating surface.

Actions to take: 

  • Inspect for plugged or restricted ejector passages and hoses.
  • Clean and remove debris, or replace the component.

Troubleshooting Example 2: DTC P04F00 caused by restriction (blocked port) 

Below is another example of DTC P04F00 on a Santa Fe (TMA) with a 2.5L T-GDI engine.

In this example the intercooler outlet has a plugged recirculation line port. A restriction in this area will block the boost pressure from reaching the Ejector and it will not function.

Actions to take: 

  • Inspect for plugged or restricted recirculation line port(s) (D) in the Intercooler Outlet and all related hoses.
  • Clean and remove debris or replace component.

Troubleshooting Example 3: DTC P04F00 caused by leaking check valve 

Below is an example of DTC P04F00 on a Kona (OS) with a 1.6L T-GDI engine.

In this example, the engine had a leaking check valve (E) which resulted in DTC P04F00. The leaking check valve caused air to pass in both directions allowing boost pressure to leak and pass backwards through the PCSV.

Actions to take: 

  • Inspect for leaking check valve(s) (should not pass air in both directions).
  • Replace the check valve assembly.
