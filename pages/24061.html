NATURAL IMPERFECTIONS OF STEERING WHEEL LEATHER

The leather used for wrapping the steering wheel is made from specially treated cowhide, which has flexibility due to the material characteristics and the manufacturing process.

This leather may have naturally occurring marks such as the ones shown below. These marks are not considered defects as they are characteristics of the leather obtained from the original cowhide.

IMPERFECTIONS OF STEERING WHEEL LEATHER CAUSED BY EXTERNAL INFLUENCES

The steering wheel is the part of the car which has the most contact with the driver. Exterior imperfections caused by the manufacturing process are covered under normal warranty. However, concerns caused by the driver's carelessness and/or frequent use are NOT  covered under warranty. Two types of common external influences are physical abrasions and exposure to certain chemical products.

PHYSICAL ABRASION

The steering wheel surface may be worn out by the accumulated physical/chemical friction between the user's hands and the steering wheel. The abrasion area and length of time until abrasion is evident may vary depending on the usage conditions. Frictional abrasion may occur by the user's driving habits (for example, the type and strength of grip). Like other ordinary leather products (sofa, handbags, etc.), frequent use accelerates the abrasion. Also, if the driver wears gloves to improve grip and/or protect their hands, the steering wheel surface may be more vulnerable to peeling due to the increased friction from the gloves.

EXPOSURE TO CHEMICAL PRODUCTS

Leather is vulnerable to alcohol, and chemical products containing alcohol are the major cause of leather peeling on the steering wheel. The amount of leather peeling may vary depending on the frequency and quantity of chemical products that were in contact with the steering wheel. Examples of chemical products containing alcohol or alcohol derivatives (ethanol, glycerol/glycerin or glycol) include skin lotion, sunscreen, hand cream, hand sanitizer, cosmetics and perfume/cologne.
